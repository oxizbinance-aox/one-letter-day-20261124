## The letter

**H**

## The word behind it

**HUNGOVER** — the morning after: pounding head, sunglasses indoors, a greasy
breakfast eaten in silence, every sound turned up too loud.

## Why this letter wins

The judge weighs, most important first: the letter stands for a real word
that describes the day; the word is vivid and specific; the word is funny or
surprising; the word feels true; the letter suits its word.

- **Vivid and specific.** *Hungover* is not a vague mood — it calls up a
  sharp picture at once: last night, the headache, the dark room, the
  breakfast that is the only plan. One word, a whole scene.
- **Funny in a way that lands at once.** It is the kind of report you repeat
  to a friend, half apology, half punchline.
- **True to a real day.** An honest account of an ordinary, strange day — not
  a performance. Everyone has had one, or watched a friend have one.
- **The letter suits the word.** H is the hard breath at the front of
  *hungover* — the sound of the day itself. It is not the obvious pick: the
  prompt's own examples (A for amazing, F for Friday-feeling, Z for zero
  sleep) are the vague, obvious letters; H is the sharper one.

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22) writes exactly one character — `H`, no newline, no
punctuation, nothing else — to `/output/entry.txt`. The output is
deterministic: the same single byte on every run.

```sh
node main.ts
xxd /output/entry.txt   # 48 — one byte, "H"
```
